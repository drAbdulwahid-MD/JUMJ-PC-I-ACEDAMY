Metabolic Syndrome (MetS)

MetS is a cluster of metabolic disorders that include:

  • Hypertension (high blood pressure)
  • Obesity (apple-shaped body)
  • Impaired blood glucose regulation
  • Dyslipidemia (abnormal blood fats)

MetS increases the risk of diabetes and cardiovascular diseases (CVDs). It affects over 20% of the US population.

Dyslipidemia

The hallmark of MetS, characterized by:

  • Increased free fatty acids (FFAs)
  • High triglyceride (TG) levels
  • Low HDL cholesterol
  • High small dense LDL cholesterol

Dyslipidemia is a major independent risk factor for cardiovascular diseases.

Hypertriglyceridemia

Defined as fasting triglyceride levels ≥ 150 mg/dL.

Risk factors:

  • Obesity and metabolic syndrome
  • Low physical activity
  • Type 2 diabetes
  • Dietary habits (high-carb intake)

Managing triglycerides involves a low-glycemic diet and increased physical activity.

Atherogenesis (Plaque Formation)

Atherosclerosis develops in multiple steps:

  1. LDL cholesterol enters the artery walls.
  2. LDL is oxidized by free radicals, becoming oxLDL.
  3. Macrophages engulf oxLDL, turning into foam cells.
  4. Foam cells trigger inflammation and plaque growth.
  5. Smooth muscle cells migrate, forming a fibrous cap.
  6. Platelets stick to the plaque, leading to thrombosis.
